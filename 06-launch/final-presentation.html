Module 1
Problem Hook & Value Proposition

Responses

  • Chosen path, which scenario are you committing to? (StreamLine or RouteLogic, or your own): My own initiative: Add batch retrieval feature to a part identification repository to enable fully automated engineering workflows through a shared API. The current function of the repository is a one-click, one event process.
  • Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?: If left unaddressed for 12 months, the company faces a critical drain on engineering productivity and an escalating data security risk. Engineers will continue to waste time on manual lookups, while teams deploy unapproved workarounds that bypass security protocols and create dangerous blind spots for risk assessment.
  • Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?: Facing a BOM-wide review deadline, engineers must find every part individually, retrieve its data or drawing, and cross-reference each item by hand. To keep up, they loop single lookups or fall back on shadow workarounds: scraping data from the legacy UI and sharing unencrypted CSV dumps.
  • Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.: The lack of batch retrieval capability forces engineers into time-intensive single-part lookup loops and shadow workarounds that create security visibility gaps and inflate AI computing costs.
  • Value proposition, based on the proposed initiative (Spotlight or Velocity), what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?: The optimized API provides batch retrieval that returns every valid part and accounts for every submitted part, restoring engineering velocity, removing the need for shadow workarounds, and giving AI-assisted workflows a path to scale at lower cost. One request resolves up to 1,000 parts in under 1,500 ms, with at least 75% fewer tokens per lookup as a benefit.

Cold-read your own hook

  • Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?: It is a high-stakes threat to the company's digital transformation strategy. Without programmatic automation, engineering teams cannot leverage AI-assisted workflows for equipment reviews. This traps discovery in manual processes that risk delayed deliverables and quality issues.
  • Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?: It is a systemic problem. Every bulk part workflow, whether auditing an entire bill of materials (BOM), cross-referencing assembly and schematic drawings, or running batch procurement manifests, is fundamentally blocked by the product's single-click architecture.
  • Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?: It directly removes the obstacle. Instead of a generic search improvement, it adds a sanctioned batch path that solves the sequential throughput bottleneck (up to 1,000 parts in under 1,500 ms). It also cuts LLM context cost through an automation payload, with at least 75% fewer tokens as a benefit.

  • The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done: The following:
    • Receive the BOM for the equipment review and see that it has hundreds of parts to verify.

    • Wrapper maintenance: Every engineer rebuilds this, so effort is duplicated across teams.

Aha moment

I originally reached the end of Module 5 and did not understand what I had built based on the prototype. Because of the added requirements in Module 5, I went back and retraced my path to get there. When I created the final version of the prototype, I requested the pop-up window that explains what the demo is as clarification for myself as well as the user. It is a visualization of backend functionality, not a UI to perform a workflow.

Key learnings

Module 5 changed the arc of the project from a general add-on feature to actually measuring the results. I had to go back and update content for the roadmap planning and MoSCoW to ensure the instrumentation necessary would be available. However, it likely would require at least one more sprint than originally planned to include all the necessary instrumentation. I picked up new vocabulary that is actively being used in my work environment. The PRD/prototype exercise was especially useful as that will likely be the path forward instead of Epics.
