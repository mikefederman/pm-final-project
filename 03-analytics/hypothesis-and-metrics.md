## Pre-work · Hypothesis check

- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** The optimized API provides batch retrieval that returns every valid part and accounts for every submitted part, restoring engineering velocity, removing the need for shadow workarounds, and giving AI-assisted workflows a path to scale at lower cost.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Persona friction: engineers "scrape data from the legacy UI or share unencrypted CSV dumps over insecure channels" (Module 2, shadow workarounds). Context: verifies parts for equipment reviews under delivery pressure with no sanctioned bulk or automated path. Takeaway (Module 3 wording): the shadow workaround is a symptom of the missing bulk path.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Snapshot 3: Equipment Review Engineers show 33% suspected workaround access (vs. 14% and 5% for other segments), 49% looped lookups, and a projected 0.5 to 1 hr saved per review (a review takes 1 to 2 hours). Snapshot 1: BOM-scale sessions fell from 15% to 7%; sessions abandoned after error or timeout rose from 18% to 31%; 412 anomalous bulk-access events per month. (All figures are simulated.)
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Equipment review engineer. Goal: Complete a BOM-wide equipment review on schedule without manually looking up each part. Confirmed friction: Looping through single lookups with no sanctioned bulk or automated path, leading to shadow workarounds (supported by the 49% looped and 33% suspected workaround access mix, and the 7% BOM-scale sessions).
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Equipment review engineers cannot retrieve BOM-scale part data through a sanctioned bulk or automated path, so they loop single lookups or fall back on shadow workarounds.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Expected behavior change (hypothesis): Engineers complete BOM-wide reviews through a single batch request instead of loops or scraping data from the legacy UI and CSV dumps. Mapping: Retention here means staying on the sanctioned retrieval path, and churn means abandonment to shadow workarounds. Simulated projection (the Batch Pilot has not yet run): Mo. 1 retention about +13 pts (74.7% vs. 62.0%) and about −22 pts abandonment for Equipment Review Engineers. Revenue is not the lens (internal platform); the value is hours saved, risk reduced, and cost avoided.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Share of pilot-team sessions on the batch path that submit 100+ part numbers (counted as part numbers submitted, since one request can carry up to 1,000). Baseline: 7% of authenticated legacy UI sessions with 100+ lookups (down from 15% six months ago). Target: 14%. The gap is closing when the pilot share rises from the 7% baseline toward the 14% target. Whether scraper sessions through the legacy UI are included in the 7% is not specified. Counting rule (proposal): a session counts when it retrieves 100+ part numbers, whether as legacy lookups or as part numbers submitted in batches, so the legacy path and the batch path are measured in the same unit. On the batch path, a session is one workflow run started by the engineer (assumption). Instrumentation that counts these sessions on both paths is required to read this metric and is in pilot scope. Companion signal: batch completion rate, the share of pilot batches whose response accounts for every submitted part (no silent drops). This signal is a proposal and depends on the assumed activity logging and reporting. Measured benefits (not proceed conditions): engineering hours per BOM-wide review (baseline 1 to 2 hours per review, about 5,000 to 10,000 hours annually in total; both are research-based assumptions, and the number of reviewers is unknown and varies week to week), compared with the pilot engineers' own pre-pilot review times, since time reduction is assumed from automation share until measured; and tokens per lookup (2,400 baseline, target at least 75% lower).
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Daily active engineers must not drop (baseline 8,420). Related guardrail: batch response time must stay under 1,500 ms per request of up to 1,000 parts, per the key metrics. Optional guardrail: legacy single-lookup latency (p95, baseline 620 ms), since repeated calls also degrade interactive users. The 3% growth in active engineers shows demand is healthy, and this metric protects the existing base. Daily active engineers counts the whole base, so a select-team pilot cannot move it, and it serves as a protective floor. Boundaries (proposal): batch response time over 1,500 ms is a breach; a drop of more than 2% in daily active engineers or a rise of more than 10% in legacy p95 latency (above 620 ms) triggers an investigation.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Not supported by the research or the simulated data, so the following is a proposal to adjust. Window: about 3 pilot months with a select team of engineers vetted for security. Proceed to general production release planning if: the pilot share of sessions submitting 100+ part numbers rises to at least 10% (a proposed threshold, about halfway from the 7% baseline to the 14% target), the batch completion rate shows every submitted part accounted for, and the guardrails hold. Pivot if: batches complete correctly but the pilot share stays below the proceed threshold, which would suggest the barrier is payload or workflow design rather than bulk retrieval. Stop if: batches fail to account for every submitted part, or the guardrails are breached and cannot be restored within the window. Adoption (the share of pilot engineers who use the batch path) is not an acceptance criterion. Comparison note: the 7% baseline covers the whole legacy base, while the pilot team is a vetted subset, so a control group drawn from the same team is the fair comparison. Test design (proposal): the pilot team is split 50/50 by engineer; the control group stays on the legacy path and the test group uses the batch path, for about 13 weeks (the 3-month window, more than 2 weekly cycles). The minimum detectable effect is the 10% proceed threshold (+3 points), which needs about 1,356 sessions per group at p < 0.05 (two-sided) and 80% power. Evidence floor (inference): about 100 engineers, about 50 per group, at 2.3 sessions per engineer per week (Snapshot 3). If one engineer's sessions closely resemble each other, more engineers or more weeks are needed. Team selection, InfoSec vetting of about 100 engineers, and the sample size calculator check are still to be confirmed.

I will protect daily active engineers (baseline 8,420) and batch response time under 1,500 ms, and will make a proceed, pivot or stop decision after 3 pilot months.
