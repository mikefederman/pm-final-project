- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Persona friction: “scrapes the database or shares unencrypted CSV dumps to meet deadlines.” Context: verifies parts for equipment reviews under delivery pressure with “no sanctioned bulk path.” Takeaway: “The workaround is a product failure, not a user failure.”

Snapshot 1: BOM-scale sessions fell from 15% to 7%; sessions abandoned after error or timeout rose from 18% to 31%; 412 anomalous bulk-access events per month. (All figures are simulated.)
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Equipment review engineer. Goal: Complete a BOM-wide equipment review on schedule without manually looking up each part. Confirmed friction: Looping through single lookups with no sanctioned bulk path, leading to workarounds (supported by the 49% looped and 33% suspected workaround access mix, and the 7% BOM-scale sessions).
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Equipment review engineers cannot retrieve BOM-scale part data through the sanctioned API, so they loop single lookups or fall back on insecure workarounds.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Expected behavior change (hypothesis): Engineers complete BOM-wide reviews through a single secure batch request instead of loops or scraping and CSV dumps. Mapping: Retention here means staying on the sanctioned API, and churn means abandonment to workarounds. Simulated projection if a pilot were run: Mo. 1 retention about +13 pts (74.7% vs. 62.0%) and about −22 pts abandonment for Equipment Review Engineers. Revenue is not the lens (internal platform); the value is hours saved, risk reduced, and cost avoided.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Share of sessions reaching BOM scale (100+ lookups) through the sanctioned API, or batch requests as a share of BOM-scale activity. Baseline: 7% (down from 15%). The gap is closing when this rises back toward or above 15%. Companion signal: anomalous bulk-access events falling from 412 per month.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Daily active engineers must not drop (baseline 8,420). Related guardrail: response time must stay under 1,500 ms per the key metrics. The 3% growth in active engineers shows demand is healthy, and this metric protects the existing base.

Pivot if: retention improves but workaround events do not fall (the problem may be payload, not bulk access). Kill if: no improvement in Mo. 1 retention or BOM-scale sessions after the window.
