## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role, who you are solving for (from M2):** Equipment review engineer. Verifies parts for equipment reviews under delivery pressure, with no sanctioned bulk or automated path.
- **Goal, what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Complete a BOM-wide equipment review on schedule without manually looking up each part.
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** Facing a BOM-wide review deadline with only 1:1 single-click lookup in the legacy UI, so the engineer must loop through parts one at a time (manual looping exhaustion, rate limiting, brittle error recovery).
- **Current workaround, the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** Scrapes data from the legacy UI or shares unencrypted CSV dumps to meet deadlines (shadow workarounds that bypass security protocols, tolerated while no sanctioned alternative exists).
- **Problem Hook, your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** The lack of batch retrieval capability forces engineers into time-intensive single-part lookup loops and shadow workarounds that create security visibility gaps and inflate AI computing costs.
- **Value Proposition, the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** The optimized API provides batch retrieval that returns every valid part and accounts for every submitted part, restoring engineering velocity, removing the need for shadow workarounds, and giving AI-assisted workflows a path to scale at lower cost.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Persona friction: engineers "scrape data from the legacy UI or share unencrypted CSV dumps over insecure channels" (Module 2, shadow workarounds). Context: verifies parts for equipment reviews under delivery pressure with no sanctioned bulk or automated path. Takeaway (Module 3 wording): the shadow workaround is a symptom of the missing bulk path.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Snapshot 3: Equipment Review Engineers show 33% suspected workaround access (vs. 14% and 5% for other segments), 49% looped lookups, and a projected 0.5 to 1 hr saved per review (a review takes 1 to 2 hours). Snapshot 1: BOM-scale sessions fell from 15% to 7%; sessions abandoned after error or timeout rose from 18% to 31%; 412 anomalous bulk-access events per month. (All figures are simulated.)
- **Persona, role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Equipment review engineer. Goal: Complete a BOM-wide equipment review on schedule without manually looking up each part. Confirmed friction: Looping through single lookups with no sanctioned bulk or automated path, leading to shadow workarounds (supported by the 49% looped and 33% suspected workaround access mix, and the 7% BOM-scale sessions).
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Equipment review engineers cannot retrieve BOM-scale part data through a sanctioned bulk or automated path, so they loop single lookups or fall back on shadow workarounds.
- **Strategic outcome, what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Expected behavior change (hypothesis): Engineers complete BOM-wide reviews through a single batch request instead of loops or scraping data from the legacy UI and CSV dumps. Mapping: Retention here means staying on the sanctioned retrieval path, and churn means abandonment to shadow workarounds. Simulated projection (the Batch Pilot has not yet run): Mo. 1 retention about +13 pts (74.7% vs. 62.0%) and about −22 pts abandonment for Equipment Review Engineers. Revenue is not the lens (internal platform); the value is hours saved, risk reduced, and cost avoided.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal), the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Share of pilot sessions submitting 100+ part numbers.
  -  Baseline: 7% (legacy UI sessions with 100+ lookups, down from 15%)
  -  Target: 14%
  -  Counting rule: One unit for both paths; a batch-path session is one workflow run.
  -  Companion signal: Batches that account for every submitted part.
  -  Measured benefits: Hours per BOM-wide review and tokens per lookup.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal), the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Daily active engineers must not drop (baseline 8,420).
  - Related guardrail: Batch response time under 1,500 ms per request of up to 1,000 parts.
  - Optional guardrail: Legacy single-lookup latency (p95, baseline 620 ms).
  - Boundaries: Batch response over 1,500 ms is a breach; investigate a drop of more than 2% in daily active engineers or a rise of more than 10% in legacy p95 latency.
  - Daily active engineers covers the whole base, so a select-team pilot cannot move it; it serves as a protective floor.
  - The 3% growth in active engineers shows demand is healthy.
- **Decision window, how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** About 3 pilot months (13 weeks).
  - Test: 50/50 by engineer; the control group stays on the legacy path.
  - Sample: About 1,356 sessions per group (about 100 engineers).
  - Proceed: Share reaches 10% or more at p < 0.05, every part is accounted for, and guardrails hold.
  - Pivot: Batches work, but the share stays below 10%.
  - Stop: Batches drop parts, or guardrails are breached.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence, one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on the M2 finding that equipment review engineers rely on shadow workarounds because no sanctioned bulk or automated path exists, and simulated M3 data showing 33% suspected workaround access and BOM-scale sessions falling from 15% to 7%, I believe that batch retrieval for equipment review engineers will let BOM-wide reviews complete through the sanctioned path, measured by the share of pilot sessions submitting 100+ part numbers rising from the 7% legacy baseline to a 14% target. I will protect daily active engineers (baseline 8,420) and batch response time under 1,500 ms, and will make a proceed, pivot or stop decision after 3 pilot months.
